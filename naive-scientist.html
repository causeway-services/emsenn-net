naïve scientist

A naïve scientist is a term for a human. Coined by Fritz Heider in Naïve Psychology (1958ce), it describes how humans desire knowledge, especially of causal relationships, especially of the human behavior they experience and thus seek causal analysis for that behavior, leading them to do attributing.
