This module covers classical information theory and its extensions toward a formal account of stability in coupled systems.

The classical foundation is Shannon's framework: entropy as a measure of uncertainty, mutual information as the reduction of uncertainty in one variable given another, channel capacity as the upper bound on reliable communication, and rate-distortion theory as the tradeoff between compression and fidelity.

The primary theoretical contribution of this module is *information-theoretic stability*: the property of a system that minimizes divergence between successive probability distributions over its states. Formally, stability is treated as a reward function on the Fisher-Rao manifold of probability distributions — a geometric characterization that requires no assumptions about semantics or intentionality. This framework shows that stability-seeking emerges necessarily as a consequence of entropy minimization in coupled signal systems.

This work connects directly to [cosmology](/cosmology/index.md) (where stability provides the language for black-hole thermodynamics and cosmological inference) and to [relationality](/relationality/index.md) (where information geometry provides one formal expression of the relational framework).
