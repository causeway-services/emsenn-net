Web design is the practice of shaping how information appears and behaves on the World Wide Web. It draws on graphic design, typography, [information architecture](../information-architecture/index.md), and software engineering, but its distinguishing concern is the medium itself: hypertext documents delivered over a network to devices of unpredictable size, capability, and context.

The web is a particular kind of information environment. Documents are linked, not bound. Readers arrive from search engines, social media, or direct URLs — they rarely start at a homepage. Screens range from watches to wall-mounted displays. Connections vary from fiber to intermittent mobile. Readers may use screen readers, keyboard navigation, or voice control. Web design must account for all of this.

The field's intellectual roots include Tim Berners-Lee's original vision for the World Wide Web as a universal information space, the W3C's standards work on HTML and CSS, and the accessibility movement that insists the web remain usable by everyone.
