Information architecture (IA) is the structural design of shared information environments — the practice of organizing, labeling, and arranging content so that people can find what they need and understand what they have found. The term was coined by Richard Saul Wurman in 1975, drawing an analogy to physical architecture: just as a building's structure shapes how people move through space, a knowledge system's structure shapes how people move through information.

IA addresses questions that arise in any collection of information: What categories exist? How do items relate to each other? What should be findable from where? How do labels communicate what they contain? These are design decisions with epistemological consequences — the categories you choose privilege certain ways of understanding the material and obscure others.
