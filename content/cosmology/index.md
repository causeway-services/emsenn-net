This module applies information-theoretic stability and reflexive dynamics to questions in physical cosmology. Rather than treating cosmic structures as interactions among independently-existing entities, this work examines how phenomena — the expansion rate of the universe, gravitational singularities, large-scale phase transitions — can be understood through a relational and information-theoretic lens.

The central formal tool is *information-theoretic stability*: the tendency of a system to minimize divergence between consecutive informational states, formalized as minimizing KL divergence over time. This provides a unified language for black-hole thermodynamics, the holographic principle, and cosmological inference under incomplete data.

This work is grounded in [relationality](/relationality/index.md) and its formal apparatus.
