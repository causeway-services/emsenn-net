Relational Infinity-Topos Modality

1. Abstract

We define and construct the Relational ∞-Topos Modality (RITM)*—a quadruple of adjoint, idempotent endofunctors internal to a cohesive ∞-topos—then show that this structure, once realized in the minimal presheaf model, immediately generates two internal sub-modalities: *Relational Dynamics (RD) and Relational Topology (RT). These internal modalities reproduce the RITM axioms, demonstrating the reflexivity of the construction. Finally, we show that this recursive structure admits a geometric reflection as a cohesive sheaf topos on smooth spaces. Together, these results confirm that RITM is not only consistent but self-stabilizing: every RITM contains within itself the structure needed to reconstruct RITM again.

2. Part I – Definition of the Structure

2.1. 1 Motivation

Cohesive ∞-topoi (Lawvere 1970; Schreiber 2013) encode adjoint modalities \(\Pi\dashv\Delta\dashv\Gamma\dashv\nabla\) that articulate global, discrete, and codiscrete structure. Directed homotopy (Grandis–Fajstrup 2001) introduces orientation but no internal modal chain. Modal HoTT (Shulman 2019) provides syntax for idempotent adjunctions. The Relational ∞-Topos Modality unites these frameworks, encoding directed relationality as an internal chain of commuting modalities.

Our program proceeds in three steps:

  1. Define RITM formally inside a cohesive ∞-topos.
  2. Construct a minimal model satisfying the axioms and derive RD, RT internally.
  3. Exhibit a geometric reflection of the same structure on smooth spaces.

2.2. 2 Definition

Let 𝔈 be a locally presentable cohesive ∞-topos (Lurie HTT §5.5).

2.2.1. Definition 2.1 (RITM)

A Relational ∞-Topos Modality on 𝔈 is a quadruple of adjoint idempotent endofunctors \[ \Pi^{\rightarrow}\dashv\Boxr\dashv\Diaml\dashv\nabla^{\leftarrow} \] satisfying:

  1. \(\Pi^{\rightarrow}\) preserves finite limits (forward shape);
  2. \(\nabla^{\leftarrow}\) preserves finite colimits (backward codiscretion);
  3. \(\Boxr\Diaml\simeq\Diaml\Boxr\);
  4. both composites are idempotent;
  5. the outer adjunction \(\Pi^{\rightarrow}\dashv\nabla^{\leftarrow}\) is cohesive.

2.2.2. Clarification of Cohesion

The adjunction is cohesive if:

  • the unit \(\eta_X\!:\!X\!\to\!\nabla^{\leftarrow}\Pi^{\rightarrow}X\) is an effective epimorphism,
  • the counit \(\epsilon_X\!:\!\Pi^{\rightarrow}\nabla^{\leftarrow}X\!\to\!X\) is an effective monomorphism,
  • and \(X\simeq\Pi^{\rightarrow}X\simeq\nabla^{\leftarrow}X\) iff X is directed ∞-connected.

This internalizes the cohesive pair \(\Pi\dashv\nabla\) of the ambient 𝔈, restricted to directionally coherent objects.

2.2.3. Definition 2.2 (Relational Morphisms)

For \(x,y\in𝔈\), a directed relation is \(r:x→\Boxr\Diaml y\). Composition is the modal pull–push: \[ r_2∘r_1=(\Boxr r_2)∘(\Diaml r_1), \] the unique composite commuting (up to equivalence) in \[

\begin{CD} x @>{r_1}>> \Diaml y @>{\Diaml r_2}>> \Diaml\Boxr z\\ @. @VVV @VV{\epsilon_z}V\\ @. \Boxr\Diaml y @>>{\Boxr r_2}> \Boxr z . \end{CD}

\]

2.3. 3 Immediate Consequences

Every RITM determines:

  • an orthogonal factorization system \((\Boxr\text{–stable},\Diaml\text{–stable})\);
  • the fixed-point condition \(μ_X:\Boxr\Diaml X→X\) is an equivalence iff X lies in both subtopoi;
  • and a notion of RITM-closed site RS ⊆ 𝔈, stable under all four modalities.

3. Part II – Constructive Model and Internal Recursion

3.1. 4 The Presheaf Cell as Constructive RITM

Let 𝒟 = {0→1} be the thin category; set 𝔈 := PSh(𝒟) ≃ Fun(𝒟ᵒᵖ, Spaces).

3.1.1. 4.1 Commuting Nuclei

Define forward/backward Lawvere–Tierney nuclei \(j_f,j_b\) by sieves \(S_f(0)=\{id₀,u\}, S_f(1)=\{id₁\}\) and \(S_b(1)=\{id₁,u^{op}\}, S_b(0)=\{id₀\}\). Because 𝒟 has only one non-identity arrow, pullbacks along u are unique; hence \(j_fj_b=j_bj_f\).

Let \(a_f⊣i_f⊣Π_f\) and \(a_b⊣i_b⊣Π_b\) be the reflectors/inclusions/coreflectors for Sh\(_{j_f}\), Sh\(_{j_b}\) ⊆ 𝔈.

3.1.2. 4.2 The Four Modalities

\[ \Pi^{\rightarrow}=a_f,\qquad \Boxr=i_f,\qquad \Diaml=i_b,\qquad \nabla^{\leftarrow}=Π_b . \] Then \(\Pi^{\rightarrow}\dashv\Boxr\dashv\Diaml\dashv\nabla^{\leftarrow}\).

3.1.3. 4.3 Verification and Cohesion

Hom-set adjunctions hold by Elephant C2.2.8, triangle equalities by unit–counit composition. Because af and Πb preserve covers and reflect isomorphisms, η:X→ΠbafX is an effective epi and ε:afΠbX→X an effective mono. Thus Π→⊣∇← is cohesive within PSh(𝒟).

3.1.4. 4.4 Explicit Action

For X with components (X₀,X₁) and X(u):X₁→X₀, \[ (a_fX)_0=\mathrm{coeq}(X₁\rightrightarrows X₀),\quad (a_fX)_1=X₁, \] and dually for ab. Coequalizers computed in Spaces preserve ∞-topos structure. Hence PSh(𝒟) is a constructive RITM—the relational cell.

3.2. 5 Internal Recursion: Deriving RD and RT

Within any RITM, consider the categories:

  • RD := \(\Boxr\)-stable endofunctors (commuting with \(\Boxr\)),
  • RT := \(\Diaml\)-stable endofunctors (commuting with \(\Diaml\)).

3.2.1. 5.1 Internal Adjunction

For G∈End(𝔈), define D:=af∘G, F:=if. Then ηf:G⇒F∘D yields a reflective factorization: \[ \require{AMScd}

\begin{CD} G @>{η_f}>> i_fa_fG\\ @V{}VV @VV{}V\\ F∘D @>>> i_fa_f . \end{CD}

\] Because if,af instantiate (\Boxr, \Diaml), every endofunctor decomposes as a composition of a dynamic (\Boxr-stable) and a topologic (\Diaml-stable) part.

3.2.2. 5.2 Reflexive Realization

\(\Boxr, \Diaml\) commute and are idempotent, so (RD,RT) satisfy the same axioms as the original RITM. Hence:

Theorem 5.1 (Recursive Realization). Every RITM canonically induces two internal sub-RITMs, Relational Dynamics (RD) and Relational Topology (RT), forming a biadjunction RD ⊣ RT that reproduces the RITM axioms internally.

Thus RITM is reflexive: its existence entails its own recursion.

4. Part III – Geometric Reflection

4.1. 6 Cohesive Site

Let 𝔈 := Shv(CartSp) with the cohesive chain \(\Pi⊣Δ⊣Γ⊣∇\) (Schreiber 2013). Let \(I_d=[0,1]\) with orientation.

Define: \[ \Pi^{\rightarrow}(X)=\mathrm{colim}_{I_d\to X}\Hom(I_d,X),\quad \Boxr(X)=\mathrm{Map}(I_d,X),\quad \Diaml(X)=\mathrm{Map}(X,I_d),\quad \nabla^{\leftarrow}(X)=\mathrm{lim}_{X\to I_d}\Hom(X,I_d). \]

4.1.1. 6.1 Verification

Internal Homs in Shv(CartSp) preserve finite (co)limits (Schreiber §3). Evaluation/coevaluation establish adjunctions: \[ \Hom(\mathrm{colim}_{I_d\to X}\Hom(I_d,X),Y) \simeq\Hom(X,\mathrm{Map}(I_d,Y)). \] Idempotence of \(\Boxr\): since the endpoint inclusion \(ι:∗⊔∗→I_d\) is a homotopy equivalence, \(\mathrm{Map}(I_d,\mathrm{Map}(I_d,X))\simeq\mathrm{Map}(I_d×I_d,X) \simeq\mathrm{Map}(I_d,X)\). Hence RITM holds in Shv(CartSp). This structure is the geometric reflection of the recursive RITM.

4.1.2. 6.2 Existence Theorem

Theorem 6.1 (Existence of RITMs). There exist small and large ∞-topoi—PSh(𝒟) and Shv(CartSp)—each carrying a Relational ∞-Topos Modality. The latter is the geometric reflection of the former’s internal recursion.

5. Part IV – Structural and Conceptual Consequences

5.1. 7 Closure and Equivalence

5.1.1. Proposition 7.1 (Modal Closure)

If U→X and V→Y are covering families in a RITM-closed site RS, then \(\Pi^{\rightarrow}U→\Pi^{\rightarrow}X\) and \(\BoxrV→\BoxrY\) are coverings; RS is closed under RITM.

5.1.2. Proposition 7.2 (Relational Equivalence)

For any RITM-closed RS, the following are equivalent:

  1. A natural iso \(\Pi^{\rightarrow}F\simeq\nabla^{\leftarrow}D\) exists;
  2. (\Boxr, \Diaml) form an orthogonal factorization system;
  3. Every RC is stable under composition.

Comment. (1) expresses the modal commutation principle for the derived regimes RD–RT, categorifying Lawvere–Tierney nucleus commutation.

5.2. 8 Interpretation

The presheaf model provides the algebraic cell of relational modality; its recursive closure yields RD and RT as internal RITMs. The cohesive model renders this recursion geometric, showing that directional cohesion in space and logic are two faces of the same construction. RITM thus describes a self-reflective topos-theoretic mechanism of stability: to define relation is to generate the conditions for relation to recur.

Future work includes formalization in Cubical Agda and extensions to probabilistic and enriched ∞-topoi.
