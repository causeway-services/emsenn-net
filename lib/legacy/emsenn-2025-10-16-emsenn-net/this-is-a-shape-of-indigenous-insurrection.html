This Is A Shape Of Indigenous Insurrection

This Is A Shape Of Indigenous Insurrection

I write densely because I am not supposed to exist.

The world that names me as undocumented, disabled, queer, non-white, Indigenous, crazy, criminal, was engineered to make my existence unliveable.

Within these constraints, the shape of insurrection looks different. I cannot do many of the acts commonly labeled as insurrectionary; I cannot maintain many of the identities that are insurrectionary.

So, I look toward how to practice what I know as insurrection from my position, and I find, that it is a practice of formal storytelling.

Inside formal syntaxes like mathematics and computing, I can move without being entirely seen. Every symbol I place is an degree of articulation between my existence and annhibilation. While, to readers, it might seem as affective as any other writing style, it is functional not an aesthetic: it's insurrection.

When I say I write toward formalism, I mean I literally write to encode, not narrrate, and this worlds that cannot be easily expropriated by the language and thinking of kyriarchism. Each formula I construct is a refusal to translate my body back into data for empire’s consumption.

I learned early that legibility kills. The bureaucratic gaze translates hunger into metrics, pain into policy, and spirit into pathology. By writing formally, by structuring thought through recursion and relation, I can say: no, hunger is hunger, pain is pain, and spirit. Formalism allows an expression of prayer.

Mathematics is not foreign to my people.

It was stripped from us when geometry was converted into ownership, when balance was rendered as equilibrium between colonizer and colonized.

Lakota geometry is relational: the circle is not enclosure, it is movement. Recursion is ceremony; it is how relation knows itself.

Each predicate I write recalls that: relation is law. Balance is logic. Motion is proof.

There is no separation, here, between mathematics and ceremony. Both require attention, humility, and repetition.

People sometimes ask why I don’t write more accessibly.

But ease of reading is a privilege of safety. Clarity to whom? I write densely because density protects. A clear sentence can be quoted, co-opted, neutralized. A dense one must be lived with, metabolized, approached like ceremony. The difficulty is not exclusion: it is an invitation to reciprocity.

It asks the reader to offer effort equal to what was required to survive long enough to write it.

And my formalisms are not abstractions; they are coordinates of a life lived inside constraint. The recursive structures I build model the recursive violences I endure. I do not seek freedom through the math, any more than I seek it through the structures.

It is simply, empire wants simplicity; I build complexity. Empire wants transparency; I build opacity. Empire wants a single solution; I build systems that remain alive by never resolving.

I relate, formally, through writing, because it is how to be Indigenous and insurrectionary, from my self. But it is pointedly a thing that doesn't offer positive value to the systems that sustain current ways of living.

Formal writing may be a way for a disabled undocumented Indigenous person to insurrect Indigenously.
